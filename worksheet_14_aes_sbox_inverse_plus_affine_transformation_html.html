Quick Reference — tap to expand
AES S-box construction (two stages):
Stage 1 — GF(2⁸) inverse: For \(a \ne \texttt{0x00}\), compute \(a^{-1}\) in \(\mathrm{GF}(2^8)\). By convention, \(\texttt{0x00}^{-1} = \texttt{0x00}\).
Stage 2 — Affine transformation: Let the bits of \(a^{-1}\) be \(b_0\ldots b_7\) (LSB first). Then: \[s_i = b_i \oplus b_{(i+4)\%8} \oplus b_{(i+5)\%8} \oplus b_{(i+6)\%8} \oplus b_{(i+7)\%8} \oplus c_i\] where the affine constant is \(\texttt{0x63}\), giving \((c_0,\ldots,c_7)=(1,1,0,0,0,1,1,0)\).
Key example: \(\texttt{0x53}^{-1}=\texttt{0xCA}\), \(\mathrm{SBox}(\texttt{0x53})=\texttt{0xED}\).
Special case: \(\mathrm{SBox}(\texttt{0x00})=\texttt{0x63}\) (because Stage 1 maps 0x00 to itself, and the affine constant alone gives 0x63).

C1. \(s_0 = b_0 \oplus b_4 \oplus b_5 \oplus b_6 \oplus b_7 \oplus c_0 = 0\oplus 0\oplus 0\oplus 1\oplus 1\oplus 1 = \mathbf{1}\)

C2. \(s_1 = b_1 \oplus b_5 \oplus b_6 \oplus b_7 \oplus b_0 \oplus c_1 = 1\oplus 0\oplus 1\oplus 1\oplus 0\oplus 1 = \mathbf{0}\)

These are the two LSBs of \(\texttt{0xED} = 1110\,1101_2\). ✓

Part D — Special case 0x00

D1. \(\texttt{0x00}\) — by convention, the inverse-step maps zero to zero.

D2. \(\mathrm{SBox}(\texttt{0x00}) = \texttt{0x63}\). Applying the affine transformation to all-zero bits XORs only the constant bits of \(\texttt{0x63}\), yielding \(\texttt{0x63}\) directly.

D3. True. Every other nonzero byte uses its genuine GF(2⁸) multiplicative inverse as Stage 1 input; \(\texttt{0x00}\) has no inverse and is mapped to itself by a special-case rule.

Part E — Conceptual questions

E1 sample. AES operates entirely within \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\)-based arithmetic. In \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) the only possible bit values are 0 and 1, and \(1+1=0\) — which is exactly XOR. Ordinary integer addition would produce carry bits (e.g. \(1+1=2\)), breaking the field structure. The affine transformation is defined over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\), so XOR is the correct and only valid addition operation.

E2 sample. The affine constant is \(\texttt{0x63}\), whose bits are \((c_0,\ldots,c_7)=(1,1,0,0,0,1,1,0)\). It is XORed into each output bit \(s_i\) as the final additive term. It ensures that the S-box has no fixed points at the all-zeros or all-ones positions that would otherwise simplify cryptanalysis. Concretely, \(\mathrm{SBox}(\texttt{0x00})=\texttt{0x63}\) arises entirely from this constant.

E3 sample. Both stages of the S-box are individually invertible, so their composition is invertible. Stage 1 (GF(2⁸) inversion) is a bijection on \(\{0,\ldots,255\}\): every nonzero element has a unique inverse, and 0 maps to 0. Stage 2 (affine transformation) is an invertible linear map over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) — the 8×8 binary matrix used has full rank. Because both stages are bijections, the combined S-box is also a bijection, and AES decryption can apply the inverse S-box (InvSubBytes).

E4 sample. No. The AES S-box has a precise algebraic construction: Stage 1 is multiplicative inversion in \(\mathrm{GF}(2^8)\), and Stage 2 is a specific affine transformation over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\). The result happens to be representable as a 256-byte lookup table for efficiency, but the values are fully determined by the field arithmetic and the chosen constant \(\texttt{0x63}\). The algebraic structure is transparent and published in FIPS 197; it was deliberately chosen to resist differential and linear cryptanalysis while remaining efficient to implement.
